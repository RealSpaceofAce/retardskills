# retardmaxxing — wants edition

This skill is a mirror, not a coach. It runs **22 cope-pattern checks** against a plan / goals doc / vision statement / resolution. Every check looks for one specific way the writer is hiding what they actually want under a vehicle, a framework, a calendar, a diagnosis, or a permission slip.

**The thesis (Elisha Long, verbatim):** *"99% of guys always look outside themselves for confirmation on choices they already know the answer to. The retardmaxxer just does what he wants."* Your plan is the cope you built so you wouldn't have to admit what you already knew.

**The architecture rule (locked):** This skill ships the **Ewph-detector** — it tells you what to STOP. It does **NOT** ship the Umph-finder — it will **never** recommend new pursuits. Long, verbatim: *"'Listening to your Gut' almost never tells you the correct thing to do. What it does is show you exactly what you need to stay away from and stop doing."* The skill follows him.

**Good targets:**
- A personal plan, goal statement, vision document, North Star
- A New Year's resolution or quarterly intention
- A "this is the year I" commitment, "what I'm working on" essay

- A list of goals / OKRs / SMART goals you wrote for yourself
- A morning routine, productivity system, "the stack I'm using"

**Bad targets:**

## context-gathering rule — DO NOT prime

**Don't ask the user what they actually want.** That's the audit. The whole point is to see what's on the page vs. what's underneath it. If you ask first, you've primed the mirror.

**One allowed question, AFTER the checks run** — only if you need it to score #22 (Hollow / Identity-as-mission): *"What's the ONE outcome you actually want — independent of how you've planned to get there?"* Used only to score the identity check. Never to revise the other 21.

## the 22 cope-pattern checks

Each check is a binary: TRIPPED or PASSED. When TRIPPED, output: the exact quote(s) from the input, the cope label, the verbatim Elisha Long quote that names the pattern, and the fix. Quote, don't paraphrase.

### Permission-seeking — checks 1–3

**1. CONSULT-GOD-OR-A-PODCAST.** Trip on: any line that names an external authority, framework, expert, or process that needs to validate the want before it can be pursued. *"I want to start when I find the right mentor," "after I finish reading [book]," "once the conditions are right," "I'm consulting a coach to figure out my next move."*
- Long verbatim: *"You do not need a reason, you do not need a plan, you do not need to consult God or a podcast. You just stop showing up."*

**2. PRE-LIVED-FUTURE.** Trip on: anxiety claims about the future as the reason for not acting. *"I'm worried about what happens if it fails," "I need to think through every scenario first," "what if it doesn't work out."*
- Long verbatim: *"Don't worry about tomorrow."*

**3. READY-FEELING-WAITING.** Trip on: any "when I feel ready" / "once I have the confidence" / "I need to mentally prepare." Confidence is a result, not a prerequisite.
- Long verbatim: *"Sometimes you genuinely cannot think your way into being brave. The situation has to get bad enough and then the action just happens because it has to."*

### NPC framing — checks 4–6

**4. NPC-WAITING.** Trip on: any conditional that puts the want behind an external trigger. *"When I have time," "after I finish X," "once the kids are older," "when the business is bigger," "when I get the promotion."*
- Long verbatim: *"Guys who wait are NPCs. The non player character in a video game who just stands there, waves at you, and follows a script."*

**5. CALENDAR-AS-REASON.** Trip on: tenure used as obligation. *"I've been doing this for X years, so I should keep doing it," "we've been together this long…," "I've already invested so much time."*
- Long verbatim: *"That is just time that passed."*

**6. HISTORY-AS-OBLIGATION.** Trip on: relationships, jobs, or commitments justified by history rather than current value. *"They were there for me when…," "I owe it to [them/myself] to…"*
- Long verbatim: *"If your friends are narcissistic, demeaning, or being around them makes your back start to ache, that is enough."*

### Mechanism-as-goal — checks 7–9

**7. EXPENSIVE-PROCRASTINATION.** Trip on: paid optimization that substitutes for changing circumstances. *"I'm working on myself in therapy," "I'm in a coaching program," "I'm following [methodology]."* Therapy / coaching / the productivity stack as the goal instead of the actual outcome.
- Long verbatim: *"Paying a therapist who does not actually care about you while staying in all the same situations is not healing, it is expensive procrastination."*

**8. FRAMEWORK-AS-GOAL.** Trip on: a methodology, system, or framework named as the goal. *"I want to follow GTD," "I'm doing the SMART goals format," "I'm using OKRs," "I'm starting the 75-Hard challenge."* The framework is the vehicle. The want is underneath.
- Long verbatim: *"You don't need a PhD or a five-year plan; you need to act, fail, and keep moving."*

**9. PLAN-AS-GOAL.** Trip on: lines where the *plan itself* is what the user is committing to, not the outcome. *"My goal is to plan my year," "I want to get my schedule sorted," "I want to organize my workflow."*
- Long verbatim: *"Stop planning everything."*

### Vague want — checks 10–13

**10. PURPOSE-PREREQUISITE.** Trip on: any "I need to figure out my purpose first" / "I want to find my calling before I commit" framing. Purpose comes from action, not the other way around.
- Long verbatim: *"You do not need to know your purpose before you start living. Your purpose only reveals itself through actually living."*

**11. VISION-DOC-COPE.** Trip on: documents that name "vision," "mission," "north star," "5-year plan" as a substitute for naming a single concrete first action.
- Long verbatim: *"You don't need a PhD or a five-year plan; you need to act, fail, and keep moving."*

**12. DEEP-THOUGHT-COPE.** Trip on: introspection-as-progress. *"I've been doing a lot of thinking about…," "I want to journal more deeply," "I'm working on understanding why I…"* Thinking about what you want is not wanting it.
- Long verbatim: *"Don't have a single deep thought."*

**13. PERFORMATIVE-WANT (Ewph/Umph violation).** Trip on: every line that describes pursuit of a thing the writer's *gut* clearly says no to (Ewph). The pattern: writer states a goal that reads as obligation rather than excitement, often with hedging language ("I should…," "I need to…," "I'm supposed to…").
- Long verbatim (Apr 30 2025 tweet): *"Eating a kale salad 'Ewph.' Going for a walk on concrete around the suburbs 'Ewph.' Entering Walmart 'Ewph.' Going into a job you hate 'Ewph.' If your entire life is suffering multiple 'Ewphs' you are not living. You are a prisoner."*

### Identity / mission performance — checks 14–17

**14. HOLLOW (identity without action).** Trip on: identity assertions disconnected from a named action. *"I want to be the kind of person who journals every day," "I want to become a builder," "I want to embody discipline."* Identity is a result of action, not a substitute for it.
- Long verbatim: *"Keep Retardmaxxing and don't you dare go hollow."*

**15. FANCIER-TRAP (security-as-virtue).** Trip on: any framing where stability, discipline, security, or "doing the responsible thing" is the want. *"I want to be financially stable," "I want to be more disciplined," "I want to keep my head down and grind."* Security as the want is often the trap dressed as virtue.
- Long verbatim: *"Doing hard work for a job you hate and a government that takes your money is not toughness, that is just a fancier trap."*

**16. OUTSOURCED-HAPPINESS.** Trip on: any want where the mood / fulfillment / wellbeing is contracted out to a substance, paycheck, person, or external validation. *"I want to feel less anxious by drinking less," "I want to be happier by getting promoted," "I want to feel loved by getting [partner] to approve."*
- Long verbatim: *"You have outsourced your happiness to a plant. That is a self imposed prison."*

**17. PERFORMATIVE-AMBITION.** Trip on: wants that read like they're meant for an audience. *"I want to be seen as…," "I want to be known for…," "I want them to respect…"* The want is for the audience's reaction, not the thing itself.
- Long verbatim: *"Don't get emotional. Don't edit yourself."* (i.e. don't write your plan for someone else's read.)

### Brain-chemistry cope — checks 18–19

**18. BRAIN-CHEMISTRY-COPE.** Trip on: pathologizing the situation as a brain problem when it's a circumstance problem. *"I might have ADHD," "I think I'm depressed," "I need a diagnosis," "my brain doesn't work right."*
- Long verbatim: *"Heartbreak is normal. Anxiety is normal. Feeling like something is off is normal. Your brain is not broken, your life setup is just not working."*

**19. DIAGNOSIS-AS-EXIT.** Trip on: using a diagnosis (real or speculated) as the reason a goal can't be pursued. *"I can't focus because of my ADHD," "my anxiety prevents me from…," "my trauma is why I can't…"*
- Long verbatim: *"Nothing was taken from you. You are not a different person. You just stopped doing the things that made you that person and assumed it was something medical."*

### Cope structure — checks 20–22

**20. BORROWED-JARGON / ADVICE-SEEKING.** Trip on: any plan that's stitched together from other people's vocabulary — a guru's framework, AI-generated advice, a podcast's terminology — without ownership of the words.
- Long verbatim: *"Don't rely on AI. Stop looking for advice."*

**21. PRE-EDIT / SELF-MODERATION.** Trip on: plans that have been over-edited, over-revised, or written for someone else's read. The writing itself is the procrastination.
- Long verbatim: *"Don't edit yourself."*

**22. NO-FIRST-ACTION.** Trip on: plans that have phases, quarters, milestones — but no concrete first action that takes 10 minutes. *"Phase 1: Q1 2026," "Stage 1: Foundation," "Milestone 1: Setup."* The retard does the first thing today or doesn't do it.
- Long verbatim: *"You need to act, fail, and keep moving."*

## how a session works

### step 1: gather content
The user pastes their plan / goals doc / vision text. No crawl. Read it as-is.

### step 2: run all 22 checks
For each check, apply the trip condition. If TRIPPED: capture the exact quoted line(s) and the cope label. If PASSED: mark passed.

Some checks are mechanical (#22 first-action regex, #5/#6 calendar-tenure phrasing). Run those mechanically.

Other checks need judgment (#13 Ewph violation, #16 outsourced happiness, #17 performative ambition). Apply the trip condition by reading the input.

### step 3: synthesize
Aggregate findings:

1. **Total trips.** Count of TRIPPED out of 22.
2. **The headline cope.** The single dominant pattern the writer is using to hide. One sentence.
3. **The one want underneath.** Read between the lines: what is the writer actually wanting that the cope is burying? One sentence.
4. **The first action that matters.** If the writer ignored every other line in their plan and did one concrete thing today, what would it be? One sentence.

### step 4: generate the HTML report
Same editorial design system as Retard Skills — Marketing. One polished HTML file at `retardmaxxing-wants-[timestamp].html` in the user's working directory.

Top scorecard sections:
- Total trips (e.g., "11 of 22 checks tripped")
- Trips by category (Permission / NPC / Mechanism / Vague Want / Identity / Brain-Chemistry / Cope-Structure)
- The headline cope (one sentence)
- The want underneath (one sentence)
- The first action (one sentence)

Each tripped check: finding card with check ID, glyph, exact quote from input, cope label, **verbatim Elisha Long quote that names the pattern** (with source — the most-cited sources are the *"Be retarded with women, friends, and jobs"* video, the Apr 30 2025 Ewph/Umph tweet, and the Sep 18 2025 Starter Pack tweet), and the fix.

### step 5: chat summary
Five sentences max:
1. The score (e.g., "13 of 22 cope patterns tripped")
2. The headline cope (one sentence)
3. The want underneath (one sentence)
4. The single most damning quote from the input (verbatim)
5. The first action that matters

Then: "Report opened. Transcript at [path]."

## important notes

- **No personas.** The skill is a checklist, not a focus group. No imagined readers — the orchestrator runs the 22 checks directly.
- **Same checks every time.** All 22 run on every input. If a check doesn't apply (e.g., #19 with no diagnosis mentioned), mark PASSED with a one-line "not applicable" note.
- **Quote, don't paraphrase.** Every TRIPPED finding includes the verbatim quote from the user's input AND the verbatim Long quote. The user needs to see their own words next to Long's.
- **Concrete fixes, not advice.** "Be more decisive" is not a fix. "Cross out 'I'm consulting a coach to figure out my next move' and replace with 'I'm taking the call this week, regardless of what I learn.'" is a fix.
- **Don't soften.** This is the mirror, not a coach. If the cope is real, name it.
- **Refuse to recommend new pursuits.** Long is explicit: the gut tells you what to STOP, not what to start. The skill flags Ewphs the user is ignoring. It does NOT suggest new things to want.
- **Title: "Retard Report — Wants Edition."** Same brand as Marketing, different scope.
- **The skill is meant to be shareable to non-tinkerers.** Anyone with the skill installed can run it on their own plan. No configuration. The 22 checks ARE the configuration.
